## Dedication

For readers building a calmer, more sustainable relationship with food, one practical portion at a time.

## Introduction

Protein-forward meals can help make smaller portions feel more complete. This book combines familiar American grocery ingredients, moderate preparation times, and flexible serving sizes. The emphasis is not perfection. It is a repeatable pattern: begin with protein, add produce, include a measured source of carbohydrate or fat, eat slowly, and stop when comfortably satisfied.

Many people taking a GLP-1 medication prefer smaller meals, softer foods, and less fat during periods of reduced appetite or digestive sensitivity. Tolerance differs from person to person and can change over time. Use the recipes as adaptable frameworks and follow your clinician's advice.

## How to Use This Book

1. Choose a recipe that fits your current appetite and tolerance.
2. Treat the listed serving as a starting point, not a requirement.
3. Eat protein first when that matches your care plan.
4. Slow down, chew thoroughly, and pause between bites.
5. Store the remaining portion promptly for another meal.
6. Adjust spice, fat, fiber, and texture when your stomach is sensitive.

## Portion and Protein Guide

- A practical meal target for many adults is 20 to 40 grams of protein, but personal needs vary.
- One palm-size portion of cooked poultry, fish, lean meat, tofu, or tempeh is usually 3 to 4 ounces (85 to 113 g).
- One cup of Greek yogurt or cottage cheese often supplies 20 to 28 grams of protein, depending on the brand.
- Increase protein gradually if large amounts feel uncomfortable.
- Sip fluids between meals if drinking with food worsens fullness, following clinical guidance.

## Portion-Smart Pantry

Keep Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, eggs, frozen seafood, cooked chicken, lean ground turkey, tofu, canned beans, reduced-sodium broth, frozen vegetables, oats, quinoa, brown rice, herbs, lemons, and mild seasonings available. These staples make it easier to assemble a small meal before fatigue or nausea turns cooking into a burden.

## Meal Prep and Food Safety

Cook poultry and leftovers to 165 F (74 C), ground meats to 160 F (71 C), and whole cuts of beef, pork, lamb, and fish to a safe temperature appropriate for the food and personal health needs. Refrigerate perishable food within two hours, use most cooked leftovers within three to four days, and discard food with uncertain storage history.

### 1. Spinach-Feta Egg Cups

This portion-smart recipe pairs eggs and egg whites with spinach and feta in a practical, protein-forward serving.

**Prep time:** 15 minutes  
**Cook time:** 25 minutes  
**Total time:** 40 minutes  
**Servings:** 4  
**Serving size:** 1/4 of recipe

**Ingredients**

- 4 large eggs
- 1 cup (240 ml) egg whites
- 2 cups (about 250 g) spinach
- 1/2 cup (about 60 g) feta
- 1/2 teaspoon fine salt
- 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
- Nonstick cooking spray

**Directions**

1. Preheat the oven to 375 F (190 C). Coat a 12-cup muffin pan with cooking spray.
2. Whisk the eggs and egg whites, then fold in the spinach and feta. Season with salt and pepper.
3. Divide evenly among the muffin cups and bake until set, 18 to 22 minutes.
4. Cool for 5 minutes. Serve three mini cups per portion.

**Estimated nutrition per serving:** 210 calories | 24 g protein | 6 g carbohydrate | 10 g fat | 2 g fiber | 410 mg sodium

**Storage:** Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days.

**Variation:** Replace the primary protein with a similar lean option or choose a lower-sodium seasoning. Individual tolerance varies, so reduce rich, spicy, or fibrous additions as needed.

### 2. Blueberry-Lemon Protein Oats

This portion-smart recipe pairs Greek yogurt and oats with blueberries and lemon zest in a practical, protein-forward serving.

**Prep time:** 15 minutes  
**Cook time:** No cooking required  
**Total time:** 15 minutes  
**Servings:** 4  
**Serving size:** 1/4 of recipe

**Ingredients**

- 2 cups (480 g) Greek yogurt
- 1 cup (90 g) rolled oats
- 1 cup (about 150 g) blueberries
- 1 teaspoon lemon zest
- Pinch of fine salt
- 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened milk
- 1 tablespoon chia seeds

**Directions**

1. Stir the Greek yogurt and oats, milk, chia seeds, blueberries, lemon zest, and salt in a covered bowl or four jars.

**Storage:** Refrigerate leftovers in an airtight container for up to 3 days.

### 13. Strawberry Cheesecake Smoothie

This portion-smart recipe pairs nonfat Greek yogurt with strawberries and vanilla and lemon in a practical, protein-forward serving.

**Prep time:** 5 minutes  
**Cook time:** No cooking required  
**Total time:** 5 minutes  
**Servings:** 2  
**Serving size:** 1/2 of recipe

**Ingredients**

- 2 cups (480 g) nonfat Greek yogurt
- 1 cup (about 150 g) strawberries
- 1 teaspoon vanilla
- 1 lemon, zested and juiced
- 1 cup (240 ml) unsweetened milk or cold water
- 1 scoop (25-30 g) protein powder
- 1 cup ice
- Pinch of fine salt

**Directions**

1. Add the nonfat Greek yogurt, strawberries, liquid, protein powder, vanilla and lemon, ice, and salt to a blender.
2. Blend until completely smooth, 30 to 45 seconds.
3. Add 1 to 2 tablespoons water if a thinner consistency is preferred.
4. Divide between two small glasses.

**Estimated nutrition per serving:** 260 calories | 30 g protein | 30 g carbohydrate | 3 g fat | 5 g fiber | 190 mg sodium

**Storage:** Best served immediately; refrigerate without ice for up to 24 hours.

**Variation:** Replace the primary protein with a similar lean option or choose a lower-sodium seasoning. Individual tolerance varies, so reduce rich, spicy, or fibrous additions as needed.

# Two-Week Shopping Framework

## Proteins

Choose amounts based on the recipes selected: chicken breast, lean ground turkey, turkey tenderloin, salmon, white fish, shrimp, canned tuna, lean beef, pork tenderloin, eggs, Greek yogurt, cottage cheese, tofu, tempeh, lentils, beans, and shelled edamame.

## Produce

Stock spinach, romaine, cabbage, broccoli, cauliflower, zucchini, peppers, onions, carrots, celery, mushrooms, tomatoes, cucumbers, green beans, sweet potatoes, lemons, limes, berries, apples, bananas, oranges, peaches, mango, and fresh herbs. Frozen produce is an equally practical option when unsweetened and unseasoned.

## Grains and Pantry Items

Keep oats, quinoa, brown rice, barley, whole-grain bread, whole-wheat pasta, corn tortillas, reduced-sodium broth, crushed tomatoes, marinara, olive oil, Dijon mustard, vinegar, herbs, spices, chia seeds, nuts, and protein powder.
